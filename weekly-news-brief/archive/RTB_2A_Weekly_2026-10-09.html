Top of mind10 · 09

The AR-15 case led every day of the week, and the “convertible pistol” fight split in two: Connecticut’s law is on hold, Maryland’s is in force. The most useful date on the board is not a court date — it is Massachusetts’s 28 October registration start.

What stayed hot. The Supreme Court’s AR-15 cases (Viramontes / Grant) topped the board on all five readings (week average 76, hot 5/5, peak 79, edging up). Connecticut’s blocked “convertible pistol” law held second (74, 5/5, peak 81) while cooling every day without an appeal, and Massachusetts Question 9 held third (68, 5/5) on a wave of voter explainers. The 5 October order list (67) and the Oklahoma Guard member’s D.C. carry charge (67, 3/5) rounded out the top five. Nothing reached volcanic for a nineteenth straight daily run.

What moved. Monday’s order list denied all 48 gun petitions it acted on, including the carry-reciprocity case Johnson v. Jacobson; Maryland’s Novotny v. Moore went to today’s conference. Also Monday, a federal judge in Maryland issued a written opinion declining to block that state’s convertible-pistol ban — the opposite result from Connecticut a week earlier. The Justice Department asked the court to dismiss the under-21 handgun-sale case as moot. And this morning FPC moved for a preliminary injunction in its Denver nonresident-carry suit.

Why it matters for RTB. Members in three states have something concrete to plan around: in Massachusetts, registration and serialization requirements begin 28 October (the state’s own firearms page says so), six days before voters decide Question 9; in Connecticut the pistol law is not being enforced while the injunction stands; in Maryland it is. Separately, a 1 October ISD report found more than $3.1 million in Meta ads selling a “national concealed carry permit” that does not exist — a trust story where a straight answer is the whole product. Draft now: a Massachusetts “what 28 October means for you” explainer, a two-state convertible-pistol status card, and a “how to spot a fake permit offer” post.

Moved ▶ DOJ · under-21 handgun sales Wk avg65↓ Mootness brief filed Mon 10/05

The Justice Department now asks a court to call the under-21 handgun-sale case moot, saying it will not enforce the ban “against anyone, anywhere”

The Office of Legal Counsel concluded on 17 September that the federal ban on licensed dealers selling handguns to 18-to-20-year-olds is unconstitutional. This week the follow-through reached the courtroom: The Reload reports DOJ filed a brief on Monday 5 October in the Firearms Policy Coalition / Second Amendment Foundation challenge asking that the case be declared moot because no live controversy remains, and TTAG quotes the brief as saying non-enforcement applies “against anyone, anywhere.” The two outlets describe the forum differently (The Reload: a federal judge; TTAG: the Fifth Circuit); the brief itself was not read here. What has not changed: the statute is still on the books, and the Federal Register shows no ATF document since 6 October, so there is still no published dealer guidance. Counter-framing: The Trace’s 30 September analysis describes a year of DOJ positions it characterises as treating gun owners as a protected class.

Watch D.C. · reciprocity · charged, pending Wk avg67↓ First appearance 09/29 · no next date reported

An Oklahoma Guard member deployed to D.C. was charged with a felony for carrying a gun that is legal to carry at home — the clearest reciprocity lesson of the week

AP reports that an off-duty Oklahoma National Guard specialist on the D.C. deployment was stopped by U.S. Park Police for an expired tag; officers saw an empty holster, he confirmed a pistol was in the vehicle, told them Oklahoma is an open-carry state, and acknowledged he had no D.C. carry licence. He was charged with carrying a pistol without a licence, made a first appearance in D.C. Superior Court on 29 September and was released. AmmoLand frames it as a national-reciprocity case study. The charge is an allegation; the case is pending, and no next hearing date was found this week. We do not name the individual.

Moved ▶ Massachusetts · Question 9 · registration Wk avg68↓ Registration starts 10/28 · vote 11/03

Massachusetts voters decide the 2024 gun law on 3 November — but its registration and serialization requirements begin six days earlier, on 28 October

Question 9 is a referendum on the 2024 law (H.4885): a “yes” keeps it, a “no” repeals it. This week brought explainers from WBUR, GBH, CommonWealth Beacon, Tufts and the Bay State Banner. The finding that matters for members came from the state itself: Mass.gov’s Firearms Services page (read today) says individuals must comply with the law’s registration and serialization requirements beginning 28 October 2026, that both systems are live in the MIRCS Unified Gun Portal, and that firearms already registered or already carrying a valid serial number need not be redone. WBUR describes the same date as the deadline to register and to request serial numbers. Per WBUR, repeal would roll back the 2024 additions but would not undo the state’s earlier licensing, training and assault-weapons rules; both campaigns’ arguments are summarised there. The law also faces court challenges, so a “yes” result does not settle every provision.

California’s new gun laws held all week; the one read in full — SB 948 — phases in over 2028 and 2029

New Colorado / Denver · nonresident carry Wk avg63→ Complaint 09/29 · PI motion 10/09

FPC asks a federal court to let visitors carry in Denver on the same terms as Colorado residents

In Hoffman v. City and County of Denver (D. Colo., filed 29 September), FPC argues that visitors from states Colorado does not recognise have no lawful way to carry in Denver: the state issues permits only to residents and recognises only reciprocal states’ permits, and Denver bans open carry. Today FPC moved for a preliminary injunction asking that plaintiff Gene Hoffman and similarly situated nonresidents be allowed to obtain Colorado permits on residents’ terms, raising Second Amendment and Article IV Privileges and Immunities claims; its release also points to the federal school-zone rule that turns on a state-issued licence. The suit follows the NRA- and CSSA-backed Howeth case filed 31 August. ConcealedCarry.com notes that a win would likely look like a permit path, not reciprocity. No response from the city or state has been reported.

Moved ▶ Convertible pistols · CT & MD Wk avg74↓ CT: PI 09/30, no appeal · MD: PI denied 10/05

Two courts, two answers: Connecticut’s “convertible pistol” law is on hold, Maryland’s is in force

Connecticut: on 30 September U.S. District Judge Kari A. Dooley preliminarily enjoined Public Act 26-41 (HB 5043) in the suit brought by Glock, Shadow Systems, NSSF and a Connecticut range — the first injunction against one of the new “Glock ban” laws. Everytown welcomed the Maryland ruling; NSSF welcomed Connecticut’s. Neither opinion was read here.
