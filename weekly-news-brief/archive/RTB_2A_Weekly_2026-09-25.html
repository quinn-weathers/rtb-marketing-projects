Top of mind · the week09 · 25

A week about buying, not carrying. The under-21 dealer-sale opinion, DOJ’s Minnesota permit-to-purchase letter, Washington’s 2027 permit and Friday’s 6–5 Hawaii ruling all concern the steps before a gun goes home — and every one of them is a posture, a letter or a ruling, not a new law.

What stayed hot. The Office of Legal Counsel’s under-21 handgun opinion ranked first or second on four of five days (avg 80, hot 5/5), firmed up midweek when a second outlet published it and linked the opinion on justice.gov. DOJ’s pre-suit letter to Minnesota (avg 75) and the Supreme Court’s December AR-15 cases (avg 75) never left the top five. The NFA non-appeal cooled steadily (82 → 68) while members waited on ATF paperwork — and on Thursday ATF’s director told an industry audience that guidance is “close” and could arrive within a couple of weeks (single outlet; nothing published in the Federal Register as of this morning). No reading reached the volcanic tier all week (ten consecutive runs).

What the law says today. 18 U.S.C. §§ 922(b)(1) and (c)(1) are unchanged on the books; the OLC opinion is an enforcement position, not a court ruling. The NFA injunction in Silencer Shop Foundation v. ATF protects the named parties, not the public. Minnesota’s permit rules are in force; its deadline passed 09/23 with nothing filed. Washington’s permit takes effect 1 May 2027. The Ninth Circuit ruling lets two Hawaii rules stand and binds only federal courts in nine western states.

What to plan around. Monday’s long conference (docket check this morning: Johnson v. Jacobson is distributed for 09/28; Novotny v. Moore was rescheduled on 09/24), the ATF fingerprint/photo comment window closing Monday 10/05, and the NFA guidance whenever it lands. Marketing’s job this week: pre-draft the explainers so they publish the hour the paperwork does.

DOJ’s Minnesota letter held the top five all week — and its deadline came and went with nothing visible, which is the expected outcome

On 17 September the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division sent a pre-suit letter to Minnesota’s governor and the mayors of Minneapolis and St. Paul, attacking delay in the state’s purchase-permit process rather than the permit requirement itself, and set a 23 September response date. No complaint, docket entry or DOJ announcement has reached this board since. Minnesota’s permit rules are in force; nothing is enjoined. Minnesota sits in the Eighth Circuit, so Friday’s Ninth Circuit ruling does not govern it. Counter-framing: no Minnesota official, Everytown or Giffords response was reachable to this board this run; that is a gap in sourcing, not evidence of silence.

Moved ▲ Washington · permit-to-purchase 2027 Wk avg71→ effective 1 May 2027 · law unchanged

Washington’s 2027 permit held steady all week — and Friday’s Hawaii ruling is now the framework any federal challenge there would meet

Nothing in Washington’s law changed: the permit-to-purchase requirement takes effect 1 May 2027, the State Patrol’s published fee is $126 with certified training priced separately, and certified training also becomes required for concealed pistol licence renewals. What changed is the legal backdrop. On 22 September the en banc Ninth Circuit, 6–5 in Yukutake v. Lopez, upheld Hawaii’s 30-day permit validity limit and pre-possession police inspection, treating objective “shall-issue” regimes as presumptively constitutional under Bruen’s footnote nine — while listing “lengthy wait times, exorbitant fees” among things that could cross the line. The majority framed the Hawaii rules as “modest, procedural burdens”; five dissenters argued acquisition is covered by the Amendment’s text and Hawaii offered no matching historical analogue. Washington is inside the Ninth Circuit. No challenge to Washington’s law has been reported.

03CATF, courts & regulatory — sustained heat

Rules, rulings, dockets
Moved ▲ DOJ · OLC opinion · 18 U.S.C. 922(b)(1) Wk avg80↓ second outlet 09/23 · ATF guidance not published

The under-21 handgun opinion was the week’s most durable story — confirmed by a second outlet, with a court case reportedly paused in its wake

The Justice Department’s Office of Legal Counsel concluded (published 17 September) that 18 U.S.C. §§ 922(b)(1) and (c)(1) violate the Second Amendment as applied to otherwise law-abiding 18-to-20-year-olds and “may not be enforced to impose criminal penalties on dealers” who sell them handguns or handgun ammunition. The Reload’s 23 September report linked the opinion on justice.gov and put an Assistant Attorney General on record; it is explicit that OLC opinions are non-binding and do not repeal statutes. FPC, a plaintiff in Reese v. ATF, says DOJ sought and obtained an extension right after the opinion issued — plaintiff’s account; the docket was not reached. Related context surfaced by this week’s research: a divided Fifth Circuit panel on 18 September struck the federal ban on direct interstate handgun purchases in Elite Precision Customs v. ATF (single outlet, docket not reached; not previously tracked by the daily board). No ATF dealer guidance appears in the Federal Register as of 09/25; atf.gov itself was not reachable.

Watch ○ NFA · Silencer Shop Foundation v. ATF Wk avg75↓ relief party-scoped · ATF says guidance close

The NFA non-appeal cooled all week while members waited on paperwork — then ATF’s director said guidance could arrive within a couple of weeks

The administration directed DOJ not to appeal Silencer Shop Foundation v. ATF, in which a Northern District of Texas judge held the NFA’s regulatory provisions unconstitutional as applied to suppressors, SBRs, SBSs and AOWs, following the $0 tax set by § 70436 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The injunction protects the named parties, not the public. New Thursday (AmmoLand, 24 September, reporting from an FFL compliance event in Dallas): ATF Director Robert Cekada said implementation guidance is “not done, but it is close,” that ATF is one of several stakeholders, and that he expects release “in the next couple of weeks.” The same report says ATF does not plan to make the injunction universal, that the guidance is expected to address SBR/SBS transfers, interstate travel with covered items and state-law interplay, and that dealers who transferred suppressors on a Form 4473 will not be cited. Unverified beyond one outlet — atf.gov was unreachable and nothing has been published in the Federal Register as of 09/25.
