Top of mind10 · 16

The NFA change was the story of the week: ATF stopped requiring registration for suppressors and short-barreled guns nationwide, and the states that tie their own laws to that federal paperwork started answering what it means for them. Late Thursday, after this week’s readings closed, a school shooting in Bend, Oregon changed the tone of everything that comes next.

What stayed hot. ATF’s nationwide non-enforcement policy for suppressors, short-barreled rifles and shotguns and “any other weapons” led all five readings (week average 87, hot 5/5, peak 94, sharply up from the Friday it broke). Its follow-on, the state-law question, entered Tuesday and climbed every day after (79, 3/5, peak 82): Firearms Policy Coalition asked 15 attorneys general to stop enforcing state laws that depend on federal registration, and by Thursday Mississippi’s attorney general, a Florida state attorney and Alaska’s Department of Law had each answered in their own way. The Third Circuit’s full-court felon-ban ruling was the week’s other climber (72, 2/5), and Massachusetts Question 9 held steady in the low 70s all week.

What faded. Connecticut’s “convertible pistol” case, last week’s number two, dropped off the board by Wednesday with no appeal filed; the scam “national carry permit” ad story ran three days and stopped; last week’s D.C. Guard carry arrest got a single Friday reading.

After the window closed. On Thursday afternoon a 16-year-old forced his way into Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon and killed three children, ages 5, 8 and 11, and a teacher; others were hurt, including a 7-year-old and a second teacher (NYT live updates). Police say a community member shot and killed the attacker within minutes; several outlets report he held a concealed handgun license, which we have not confirmed with police. AP, NBC, The New York Times and The Washington Post all carry the core facts. This is not in the week’s heat readings, but it will dominate next week’s gun-policy coverage.

Why it matters for RTB. Two different jobs this week. For the NFA story, members need a calm “federal paperwork vs. your state’s law” explainer, because the answer now differs state by state. For Bend, the right move is restraint: hold promotional social through the weekend, keep the event out of any ad or offer, and let the facts settle. Pure peace of mind means being steady when the news is not.

03AFederal legislation — sustained heat

Bills, letters, campaign money — and the federal executive positions (DOJ, Park Service) that change how existing federal law is enforced
Moved ▶ DOJ · under-21 handgun sales Wk avg64→ House letter 10/08 · mootness brief pending

174 House members ask DOJ to reverse course on under-21 handgun sales; DOJ keeps asking the court to call the case moot

The Office of Legal Counsel concluded in September that the federal ban on licensed dealers selling handguns to 18-to-20-year-olds is unconstitutional, and DOJ told the court it will not enforce it “against anyone, anywhere.” This week, a letter from 174 House members, released by the House Gun Violence Prevention Task Force and backed by Giffords, asked DOJ to “immediately rescind” that opinion, arguing the decision belongs to Congress. The Reload’s 10/15 analysis adds a twist: in the same filing (Reese v. ATF), DOJ argued that any injunction should cover only actual plaintiffs and existing members, which is the opposite of how the NFA injunction was written. Where things stand: no court ruling on mootness; no ATF dealer guidance in the Federal Register. Many states set their own minimum ages, and those are unaffected.

03BState legislation & ballot measures — sustained heat

State answers to the NFA change, ballot measures, and county-level carry-permit fights
New NFA · state “mirror” laws Wk avg79↑ FPC letters 10/09 · 3 public answers by Thu

With federal registration paused, states are deciding what their own NFA-linked laws now require, and the answers differ

Many state laws allow a suppressor or short-barreled rifle only if it is lawfully registered under federal law. FPC’s 9 October letters asked 15 attorneys general to stop enforcing those provisions now that ATF is not registering anything. Answers so far: Mississippi’s attorney general (12 Oct) called the ATF Form 4 voluntary for suppressors but noted concealed carry of a suppressor is still a state offense and said the office will seek law changes in 2027. Alaska’s acting attorney general urged the next Legislature to fix inconsistencies between state and federal law (Alaska Watchman, 15 Oct), which implies the state law has not changed yet. In Florida, a state attorney’s 11 October memo told police that missing federal registration alone is no longer evidence of a crime for SBRs, SBSs or suppressors (The Reload; memo linked). That memo comes from one prosecutor’s office; how far it reaches beyond that circuit is Unverified. Louisiana’s wildlife agency said its agents will not cite hunters solely for missing federal stamps (snippet-level). In Maine, the Bangor Daily News says over-the-counter suppressor purchases are now possible. Machine guns and destructive devices are unchanged everywhere.

03CATF, courts & regulatory — sustained heat

Agency actions, the Supreme Court docket, and the appellate rulings that set the rules members live under
Moved ▶ ATF · NFA non-enforcement Wk avg87↑↑ Effective 10/09 · Brown v. ATF status conf. 10/22

ATF will not enforce NFA registration, approval or tax-stamp requirements for suppressors, short-barreled rifles and shotguns, or AOWs, for anyone, nationwide

After the Silencer Shop Foundation v. ATF ruling (consolidated with Jensen v. ATF, N.D. Tex., Judge James Wesley Hendrix), DOJ chose not to appeal, and on 9 October ATF’s director issued an open letter and FAQ extending non-enforcement to everyone, not only the plaintiffs (AP, The Reload, SAF, NRA). Unchanged: machine guns and destructive devices; Form 4473 and a background check at a dealer; interstate transport notice (Form 5320.20) for SBRs and SBSs; and every state law. What could reverse it: Congress restoring the tax, a court narrowing the injunction, or a future administration rescinding the policy. DOJ has itself argued (10/12) that the injunction covers only plaintiff states, members and customers. A counter-signal: the Duke Center for Firearms Law highlighted United States v. Berger (3d Cir., 1 Sept.), which held NFA silencer registration is not an “infringement.” Gun-control groups’ formal statements on the change did not surface in our feeds this week; that is a gap, not evidence of silence.

New Felon ban · Third Circuit en banc Wk avg72↓ Decided 10/13 · 13 SCOTUS gun denials same day

The full Third Circuit says the federal felon gun ban cannot be applied to a man over two decades-old DUI convictions, but can be for a violent record

In Williams v. Blanche, an FPC-backed case, the en banc Third Circuit held the lifetime ban under 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) unconstitutional as applied to a Pennsylvania man whose only predicate was a 2005 DUI. Hoodline, citing Reuters, reports the vote as 8–6. In a companion case (Bost), the court upheld the ban for a terroristic-threats conviction, holding some predicate crimes show a prima facie risk that justifies disarmament (Volokh Conspiracy excerpt). Both sides’ framing: FPC calls it a major win; Fox News headlines that “some nonviolent felons can legally own guns.” The same day, the Supreme Court denied 13 firearms petitions, 8 of them felon-ban cases (Duke).
