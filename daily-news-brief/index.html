Top of mind10 · 22

A federal judge has struck down New York’s ban on buying body armor, ruling that bullet-resistant vests are “arms” protected by the Second Amendment. It is the first ruling we have seen that extends the right to equipment whose only job is to protect the person wearing it.

What happened. Per Courthouse News (10/21, read), U.S. District Judge John Sinatra (W.D.N.Y.) held in a 47-page opinion on Wednesday that New York Penal Law 270.21, the 2022 ban on civilian purchases of body armor, is unconstitutional. Applying Bruen, he found that body armor is an “arm” under the Second Amendment’s plain text and that the state failed to show the ban fits the nation’s historical tradition of regulation or that modern body armor is “dangerous or unusual.” The suit was brought in 2024 by the Firearms Policy Coalition and two members who said they wanted body armor for lawful self-defense. The law exempted police, peace officers, the military and other “eligible professions”; a first violation was a class A misdemeanor. The Attorney General’s office said it is reviewing the ruling.

What is not confirmed. We read Courthouse News, not the opinion. Whether the judge entered an injunction, its scope, and whether it is stayed pending an appeal to the Second Circuit were not in what we read. FPC’s statement uses heated language about state officials; we do not repeat it. About ten outlets carried it within hours (Buffalo News, WIVB, Syracuse.com, Spectrum, the Niagara Gazette, nny360, USA Carry, Outdoor Wire and FPC itself): high salience on the pickup proxy.

Also this morning. Dearborn, Michigan: a woman who has held a carry license for three decades was arrested after shooting a man who was attacking her boyfriend inside a clothing store; three days later the Wayne County Prosecutor’s Office declined to charge her, citing store video (FOX 2, read; Section 05). Industry: Smith & Wesson is buying Savage Arms for $160 million in cash and stock, creating the largest commercial gunmaker in the country (MassLive, read; Section 04). Courts: the Fifth Circuit held the Federal Switchblade Act unconstitutional (Section 03).

Why it matters for RTB. Protection is broader than a firearm. A court has now said, in plain terms, that the equipment people use to survive an attack sits inside the same right as the tools they use to stop one. That supports an empowerment frame RTB already owns: preparation is a set of choices (training, gear, and a plan for what happens afterward). Dearborn shows the “afterward”: a lawful carrier, a defense of someone else, an arrest, and three days before a decision. Run the body-armor news as a short “what the ruling says and what it doesn’t” for New York members; until the state’s next step is clear, the guidance is do not assume the ban is gone. Bend: from today the general “two tracks after a use of force” explainer may run, with no reference to the case. Not legal advice.

New ▲ Federal court · New York · body armor Heat84▲▲ Courthouse News read 10/21 · opinion not read · ~10 outlets

Judge rules New York’s 2022 body-armor ban unconstitutional, holding that protective vests are “arms” under the Second Amendment

The ruling. Per Courthouse News (read), Judge John Sinatra of the Western District of New York found that founding-era understandings of “arms” included defensive armor, that body armor is therefore covered by the Second Amendment’s plain text, and that New York failed to identify a sufficiently analogous historical tradition or to show that modern body armor is dangerous or unusual. The law. Penal Law 270.21, enacted in 2022 after the Buffalo supermarket attack, barred purchases by anyone outside police, peace-officer, military and other eligible professions; a first offense was a class A misdemeanor and a repeat offense a class E felony. The parties. Firearms Policy Coalition and two members sued in 2024; the defendants are the Attorney General, the State Police superintendent and the acting Erie County district attorney. The AG’s office is reviewing the ruling. Not in what we read: the judgment’s exact terms, any stay, or a notice of appeal. Buffalo News has posted the ruling (headline); we did not read it.

RTB angleSame right, different tool. This is the clearest court statement yet that “bearing arms” includes protecting yourself, not only stopping a threat. It fits a measured “layers of preparation” message. For New York members: the ruling can be appealed and, if stayed, the law would still apply, so the message is “watch for the state’s next step,” not “go buy a vest.” We do not name the Buffalo attacker. Not legal advice.

New ▲ Fifth Circuit · knives Reason/Volokh read 10/22 · 2 outlets

The Fifth Circuit holds the Federal Switchblade Act unconstitutional, finding switchblades are “arms” in common use

Per Eugene Volokh’s excerpt of the opinion (10/22, read), a three-judge panel decided Knife Rights, Inc. v. Blanche on Wednesday. The court held that switchblades are “arms” covered by the Second Amendment, noting “millions” of automatically opening knives in lawful use, and that the government failed to show a historical tradition of restricting the manufacture, interstate transport or distribution of concealable weapons; most historical laws it cited regulated how such weapons were carried. The Act (15 U.S.C. § 1242) carries up to five years in prison for introducing or distributing switchblades in interstate commerce. The Washington Times also reported it (headline). Whether DOJ will seek rehearing or Supreme Court review was not reported. State knife laws are unaffected by this ruling.

RTB angleA reminder that “arms” is broad. Two rulings in one day reached items that are not firearms (body armor and switchblades). For members, the practical point is narrower: a federal ruling does not change state knife or carry laws, which vary widely. A short “knives and the law: check your state” item would be accurate and useful. Not legal advice.
