The City’s 29 October release (read) says the Council voted 12–0 on 28 October to end the exception that let concealed-handgun license holders carry into buildings the City uses for official meetings, naming City Hall, the Portland Building and the 1900 Building. ATF’s chief counsel resigns; The Reload expects most of the 34-rule package to survive, but not the contested no-visit sales rule

ATF announced on 30 October that Chief Counsel Robert Leider has resigned and is expected to return to the Antonin Scalia Law School at George Mason University. The release says he joined in early 2025 and supported “more than 40 proposed and final rules” (read). The Reload’s analysis (11/1, read) describes him as the architect of the 34-rule package announced in May, still moving through rulemaking. It reports public statements from both sides describing an amicable split, but says it could not confirm the full story. Its assessment: most rules will likely be finalized because they are modest and durable; the proposal most at risk is “Revising Non-Over-the-Counter Firearms Transaction Requirements,” which would let dealers complete some sales without an in-store visit using approved online identity checks, and which drew heavy opposition from small dealers and NSSF. A rumored broader “sporting purposes” import rule, it says, probably will not come. The Truth About Guns, AmmoLand and Guns.com also reported the departure. Added 11/3: CBS News (10/30, read) reports, citing a source, that he remains an ATF employee through January but is on administrative leave from Monday 11/2; that a source says the decision was not related to DOJ’s choice not to appeal a Texas court ruling on the National Firearms Act; that earlier reports said he did not always agree with DOJ leadership on legal positions in pro-gun groups’ suits; and that the article does not say what happens to the proposals. No successor named. Update 11/4: ATF’s own news page (read) lists “ATF’s chief counsel will return to George Mason University” (30 October), consistent with the departure; no successor named and no new rule on the page through 11/3. New ▲ Sixth Circuit · §922(g)(4) · mental-health prohibition decided 10/29 · reported 11/5 · The Reload read · one outlet

Sixth Circuit rejects a Second Amendment challenge to the federal ban on gun possession by people found mentally ill and dangerous

In United States v. Laykovich (appeal from the Eastern District of Kentucky), a three-judge panel affirmed on 29 October that 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(4), which bars firearm possession by people adjudicated mentally defective or committed to a mental-health institution, does not violate the Second Amendment, at least as applied to the defendant, per The Reload (read, 11/5). The panel reasoned that the law resembles historical laws allowing the government to detain people whose mental illness made them dangerous, serves the same public-safety purpose, and lasts only until the person no longer poses a threat. The defendant had also challenged the federal drug-user prohibition, §922(g)(3); the article summary does not state how that challenge came out. According to the article the defendant was held involuntarily in 2023 and later found with multiple firearms; the article is internally inconsistent about his home state, so no state is given here. The district court had sentenced him to four years in 2025. Single outlet (The Reload); the opinion itself was not read.

RTB angleA court explaining a limit, not a change in law. The evergreen explainer on who is prohibited from possessing firearms under federal law, and how rights can be restored where a state allows it, fits here. No commentary on the ruling and no forecast of further review. Not legal advice.

Sturm, Ruger & Company’s third-quarter release (4 November; SEC exhibit, read) reports net sales of $147.6 million against $126.8 million a year earlier (up 16.5%), net income of $5.8 million, diluted earnings of $0.36 a share against $0.10, gross margin of 20.7% against 15.1%, and a $0.18 dividend. Year to date, net sales were $447 million (up 13%) and diluted earnings $0.79. The company said it suspended production of some product families, together about $25 million in revenue with minimal profit, as part of a portfolio review. The release’s adjusted-earnings figures appear inconsistent between the headline and the reconciliation table, so they are not repeated here. No independent outlet coverage was found in this run.
