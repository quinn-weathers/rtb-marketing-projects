North Carolina’s permitless-carry override did not get a vote this week; the House majority came up two votes short of three-fifths on attendance, and the next window is 30 November to 18 December.

What happened. On Tuesday 17 November the House took up none of the four vetoed bills on its calendar, SB 50 among them. With 117 members present, 70 votes were needed and the majority had 68, because three of its members were absent; the Speaker said at least one override is still likely before the term ends, and the governor urged lawmakers not to take them up (NC Newsline via Yahoo, read). No more votes are scheduled this week. This settles what Tuesday’s edition left open: current permit rules continue for now.

Also new today. Congress: a House Oversight hearing on ATF’s out-of-business records, the archive of closed dealers’ transaction records, is set for 2:00 p.m. ET today (Section 01). Texas: Fox 26 (11/17, read) names four of the roughly 35 prefiled bills, including under-21 purchase limits, private-sale background checks and an extreme-risk order bill (Section 02). Polling: Gallup finds 56% want stricter laws on firearm sales and a record 15% want them less strict (Section 06). Self-defense: Missouri prosecutors declined charges in a September road-rage shooting, saying the elements of self-defense were “insurmountable” (Section 05).

Still not reachable: the News folder on the computer (third straight run; see the footer). Publishing: the live page now shows Tuesday 11/17, so the backlog flagged yesterday has been pushed.

Why it matters for RTB. A pending bill is not a change in the law. North Carolina members have read “permitless carry” headlines for months; the accurate message today is that permits are still required and the issue could return in early December. Ohio (SB 214) is in the same place, awaiting the governor. A calm “what’s in effect today” format serves both. Not legal advice.

House Oversight holds a hearing today on how ATF keeps the records of gun dealers that have gone out of business

Moved ▶ North Carolina · SB 50 · veto override Heat78▶ House met Tue 11/17 · NC Newsline via Yahoo read · next votes 30 Nov–18 Dec

North Carolina’s House did not vote on the permitless-carry override; leadership has reserved 30 November to 18 December for one more voting session

On Tuesday the House took up none of the four vetoed bills on its calendar: SB 50 (permitless carry for most adults 18 and older, without a permit or safety training), HB 437 (encampments), HB 377 (judicial suspensions) and HB 958 (election changes). With 117 members present the override bar was 70; the majority had 68 because three of its members were absent. The Speaker said at least one override is still likely before 2027; the governor advised against taking them up. No more votes are scheduled this week (NC Newsline via Yahoo, read). WSPA, Fox 8 and WUNC carried previews (headlines). The Senate overrode the veto last year, so a House override would make SB 50 law. Democrats take the House majority in January, and current legislators’ terms end 31 December.

RTB anglePermits still required. The useful message for North Carolina members is unchanged: a permit is required to carry concealed today, and the bill could come back in early December. Keep both versions of the explainer ready. No legislator names, no scorekeeping. Not legal advice.

New ▲ Polling · Gallup · gun laws Heat72▲ Fielded 1–19 Oct · The Reload read 11/17

Gallup: 56% want stricter laws on firearm sales, down from 60%; a record 15% want them less strict

Per The Reload’s write-up (11/17, read), Gallup’s annual October survey (1,200 adults by phone, 1–19 October, ±4 points) finds 56% want firearm-sales laws made stricter, 27% kept as they are and 15% made less strict, the highest “less strict” share in Gallup’s trend. For the first time in that trend, more Republicans favor less strict laws (31%) than stricter ones (16%); half want them kept as is. Independents: 54% stricter (down from 63%), 16% less strict. Democrats: 89% stricter. FingerLakes1 carried the release on 12 November (headline). Gallup’s own page was not read.

RTB angleOpinion is shifting, and the middle is large. Over a quarter of adults want laws left as they are. Empowerment-led, non-partisan messaging (know the law, carry responsibly, have a plan) reaches that middle better than fear framing. No party scorekeeping in public-facing copy.
