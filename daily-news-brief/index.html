Top of mind10 · 09

Suppressor demand is surging, but the court ruling that removed NFA registration covers only some buyers, and DOJ is now arguing against widening it. “Am I covered?” is the question RTB members will be asking.

What happened. Per AmmoLand (10/8, read), DOJ filed a brief on 7 October in the consolidated Brown v. ATF / Missouri v. ATF case in federal court in Missouri, opposing relief that would protect every resident of the plaintiff states or gun owners nationwide. It cites the Supreme Court’s 2025 Trump v. CASA decision limiting universal injunctions, argues states cannot sue on behalf of their residents, and asks to drop five states that already won in Texas. This comes after DOJ let the 5 October deadline pass without appealing the Texas ruling (Silencer Shop Foundation, 5 August) that held NFA registration for untaxed suppressors and short-barreled rifles exceeds Congress’s power. That injunction protects the plaintiffs and their members and customers, not every American. The judge has set a status conference for 22 October. Separately, The Reload (10/8, feed summary read) reports NSSF figures, from ATF records obtained by FOIA, showing NFA registrations up 129% year over year in September and suppressor sales past 1.1 million this year.

What it is not. Not a ruling: the Missouri judge has not decided DOJ’s arguments. Not ATF guidance: AmmoLand says it expects ATF’s NFA guidance “within the next day or two,” but that is the outlet’s expectation, and the Federal Register shows no ATF documents since 6 October (checked this run). DOJ’s brief and the court’s 10/6 order were described by AmmoLand, not read here.

Why it matters for RTB. A big wave of new suppressor owners, many buying for home defense, is meeting legal uncertainty about who the ruling protects. That is RTB’s lane: know your status before you assume it. A plain-English “Does the NFA ruling cover me?” explainer (who the injunction names, what is still pending, and what to keep on file) is timely and empowering. It pairs with yesterday’s U.S. LawShield and Silencer Central NFA-trust partnership: peers are already meeting these buyers at checkout. Hold any “registration is over” language; it is not true for everyone.

New ▲ California · Contra Costa County · carry permits Heat71▲▲ settlement announced 10/8 · 5 outlets

Contra Costa’s sheriff drops bans on red-dot sights, weapon lights and 1911-style pistols for county carry-permit holders

Per the Second Amendment Foundation’s announcement (10/8, read), SAF and two county residents settled SAF v. Contra Costa County. The Sheriff’s Office will withdraw its prohibitions on red-dot sights, weapon-mounted lights, and single-action 1911 and 2011 pistols carried under a county license, and update its carry-permit website within 10 days. Applicants still have to range-qualify with any gun listed on the license, and existing licensees who add a red dot or light may need to present the gun for inspection and requalify. AmmoLand, the Daily Caller (via AOL), the Tampa Free Press and a second AmmoLand piece also covered it. The county’s own statement and the settlement text were not read.

RTB angleA practical, member-useful change, with a catch worth explaining. In California, your carry permit lists specific guns, and changing a listed gun (even adding a light) can mean requalifying. A short “what’s on your permit matters” piece fits RTB’s education-first voice. Wait for the county’s updated page before quoting new rules.

Moved ▶ Colorado · Denver · nonresident carry Heat68▲▲ PI motion announced 10/9 · 4 outlets

FPC asks a federal court to let nonresidents get Colorado carry permits on the same terms as residents, starting now

Per FPC’s release on The Outdoor Wire (10/9, read), FPC filed a motion for a preliminary injunction in Hoffman v. City and County of Denver (D. Colo.). Colorado issues concealed-carry permits only to residents and honours only permits from states that honour its own; Denver bans open carry. FPC argues that leaves some visitors no lawful way to carry in Denver, and notes a federal knock-on: the federal school-zone law exempts people licensed by the state they are in, so a visitor without a Colorado permit faces a patchwork of no-go areas statewide. The motion relies on Bruen and the Privileges and Immunities Clause. No response or hearing date reported. The motion itself was not read.

RTB angleThe federal school-zone point is the under-explained piece. Carrying near a school in another state can raise a federal question, not just a state one, if that state did not issue your permit. Educational; point members to official sources and avoid predicting the ruling.

DOJ asks a Missouri court not to extend NFA relief to all residents of the plaintiff states, or nationwide

See Top of mind. In brief, per AmmoLand (read): DOJ’s 7 October supplemental brief before Chief Judge Stephen R. Clark invokes Trump v. CASA against universal relief, disputes the states’ standing to sue for their residents, and seeks dismissal of Georgia, Kansas, Oklahoma, South Dakota and Utah because they already won in Texas. It concedes Missouri’s amended complaint appears procedurally proper. Clark’s 6 October order noted the Texas appeal deadline passed and set a Zoom status conference for 22 October on the Texas judgment’s effect, the five states’ standing and Missouri’s complaint. The Texas injunction covers the plaintiffs and their members and customers, and excludes “any other weapons” for one plaintiff group. AmmoLand expects ATF guidance within days; none is published (Federal Register checked).

RTB angle“Covered or not” is a membership-shaped question. Whether the ruling protects you can depend on which group you belong to and where you bought. Don’t tell members the injunction covers them; tell them how to find out. Revisit when ATF publishes guidance.

New ▲ Self-defense determination · Martinez, Ga. incident 10/8 · 3 outlets

A Georgia sheriff’s office says a delivery driver acted in self-defense when he shot a man who lunged into his car; no charges for now

Per The Augusta Press (10/9, read), the Columbia County Sheriff’s Office says a 26-year-old DoorDash driver was confronted twice outside a Martinez shopping center on Thursday by a man demanding he buy him food. The man shoved him, later opened his passenger door and threatened to take the car. The driver, seated, displayed a handgun and told the man to leave; the man said he would take the gun too, came around to the driver’s side and attacked him through the open door. The driver fired three times. Deputies reviewed witness accounts and security video (no audio). The sheriff’s office said that “based on the evidence obtained to this point” he acted in self-defense; no charges pending the final outcome. The wounded man was in surgery. WJBF and WFXG also reported. We are not naming anyone.

RTB angle“At this time” is doing real work in that sentence. An early no-charge decision can still be revisited as the investigation finishes, and civil exposure is a separate question. Also notable: video and witnesses carried the determination. Educational, not sensational.
