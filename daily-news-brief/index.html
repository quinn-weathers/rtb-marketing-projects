Top of mind10 · 29

About 40 briefs defending “assault weapon” bans reached the Supreme Court in a single day, from 19 attorneys general, police chiefs, doctors, teachers, and families of school-shooting victims. The AR-15 cases are argued 2 December.

What happened. The docket for Viramontes v. Cook County (25-238), read this morning, shows roughly 40 amicus briefs filed 28 October in support of Cook County and Connecticut. They include a multistate brief led by Massachusetts and New Jersey with 16 other states and the District of Columbia (reported as 19 attorneys general) and a separate Virginia brief; police groups (the International Association of Chiefs of Police, Major Cities Chiefs, the NYPD, former big-city chiefs); medical and public-health groups; teachers’ and school-nurse groups; Everytown, Giffords and Brady; families of victims of the Sandy Hook and Parkland shootings; historians and social scientists; cities and counties; and a group of current and former members of Congress. The Washington Post, Law.com and Newser led on the Sandy Hook families’ brief.

What is not confirmed. Connecticut’s own merits brief, returned for correction on 21 October, does not yet appear in corrected form. Gun-rights groups’ reply briefs follow; we have not confirmed the deadline.

Also this morning. National outlets (ABC News) have identified both the attacker and the parent who stopped him in Bend; we continue not to name either (Section 05). The Seventh Circuit heard a challenge to Illinois’ suppressor ban (Section 03). Massachusetts votes on Question 9 in five days; the registration-deadline claim is still unverified (Section 02). In New Mexico, Lea County voters will decide whether to raise a tax to fund legal challenges to state firearm laws (Section 02).

Why it matters for RTB. This is the biggest Second Amendment case in years, and members will see a lot of noise. A calm explainer (what the Court is deciding, who has weighed in on each side, when a decision is expected, what does not change in the meantime) serves members far better than reacting to individual briefs. No forecast. Not legal advice.
