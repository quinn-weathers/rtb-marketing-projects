Top of mind09 · 28

The Supreme Court’s long conference is today, and the weekend made the post-Bruen split plainer: the Ninth Circuit majority that upheld Hawaii’s permit rules said in its own opinion that it hopes the Justices will settle the footnote-nine question. No orders have been released, and no petition in the Hawaii case has been reported.

What happened. The Reload’s Sunday members’ newsletter — an empty page on Friday, read in full today — quotes all three opinions in Yukutake v. Lopez. The majority held that objective, non-abusive shall-issue rules are presumptively constitutional unless shown to be put to “abusive ends,” and found Hawaii’s 30-day permit window and police-station inspection to be modest procedural burdens. The principal dissent, joined by four judges, argues footnote nine was a limiting clarification about carry licensing, not a new test, and does not reach post-permit purchase rules at all. The majority closed by writing: “Hopefully, the Supreme Court will make clear which analysis it thinks is correct.”

What it does and does not do. Nothing in that sentence is a petition, a grant or a forecast; it is a court acknowledging that circuits disagree on method. The ruling still binds only the nine Ninth Circuit states and still changes no law. Today’s conference is the Court’s first of the term; results normally arrive in later orders lists, typically beginning with the first Monday in October (5 October, T−7). Yukutake is not before the Court; the ordinary certiorari deadline is about 21 December.

Why it matters for RTB. The permits-and-waits explainer recommended on Friday now has its cleanest possible hook: the judges themselves say the rule is unsettled. That lets RTB explain the question without taking a side on the answer — exactly the empowerment frame. Separately, a recall-layer finding: a coverage peer’s monthly news roundup surfaced four September items this board never carded, including a Delaware Supreme Court ruling reinstating under-21 restrictions (sections 02–04). They are late to this board, not new events, and are labelled that way.

Moved ▲ 9th Cir. en banc · Yukutake v. Lopez · 21-16756 Heat79▼ day 4 · dissents read in full · no filing reported

The full Yukutake exchange is now readable — and the majority itself asked the Supreme Court to settle the footnote-nine question

The Reload’s 09/27 members’ newsletter, empty on Friday and read end to end today, sets out the three opinions. Majority: across different methods, circuits have converged on treating non-abusive shall-issue laws as consistent with the Second Amendment, so the only question is whether Hawaii’s two rules were put to abusive ends; it found they were not. Principal dissent (five judges): footnote nine never says “presumptively lawful,” assigns no burden, and in any case concerns carry-licence criteria — not what happens after a permit to possess in the home is issued. Solo dissent: the majority created a carve-out Bruen does not contain. The majority acknowledged the disagreement and said it hopes the Court will clarify. No rehearing petition or certiorari filing has been reported; ordinary deadline about 21 December. The court’s own PDF link is known; this account still rests on one outlet quoting it.
