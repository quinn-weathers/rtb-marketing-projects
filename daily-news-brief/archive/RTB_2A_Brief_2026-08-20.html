Colorado’s permit-to-purchase requirement for semi-automatic firearms has been in force since August 1 and has been almost entirely crowded out by the NFA story

While every reachable outlet has spent three weeks on a federal injunction, a state-level change with immediate practical consequences for a large membership base took effect and passed nearly unremarked on this board. Under Colorado Senate Bill 25-003, since August 1, 2026 an individual wishing to purchase most specified semi-automatic firearms — including certain rifles and certain handguns with detachable magazines — must first obtain a Firearms Safety Course Eligibility Card, which requires completing a background check and a state-approved firearms safety course. That is a permit-to-purchase system for covered semi-automatics, and it is operating now. New Scope · Households · Not covered Heat87▲#2 · new finding from full opinion text

Resident family members of covered people are not covered — the judge was asked directly and declined, and no one in the category has told anyone

The single most practically useful finding of the run, and it was sitting in the part of the analysis this board had only read at dek level. The Silencer Shop plaintiffs asked that the injunction reach “resident family members” living in the same household as a covered person. Judge Hendrix declined, noting that the plaintiffs “make no argument for why the injunction should cover resident family members of any individual covered by the injunction, including, for example, a customer of a commercial member of an associational plaintiff.” Read alongside the transaction-scoping already on this board, the practical picture for a household is sharp: coverage attaches to a person through membership or through a transaction with a named plaintiff, and it does not spread across a roof. A GOA member is covered; their spouse is covered only if the spouse is independently a member or independently a customer in the transaction. The related limitation is equally sharp: Hendrix wrote that the injunction “only covers current and future customers’ transactions with the plaintiffs and their commercial members,” that the benefit to non-party customers is “merely incidental,” and that it does not let a covered customer avoid the provisions “for all NFA firearms they possess, transfer, sell, make, and manufacture.” Coverage is not a status a person carries around; it attaches to particular transactions with particular parties.
