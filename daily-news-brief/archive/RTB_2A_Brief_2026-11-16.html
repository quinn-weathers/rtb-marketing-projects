Top of mind11 · 16

The Supreme Court today declined to hear the challenge to Maryland’s “sensitive places” carry restrictions, leaving the Fourth Circuit’s ruling upholding them in place.

What happened. On this morning’s order list (Monday 16 November), the Court turned away Novotny v. Moore (No. 25-1324), brought by gun-rights groups including the NRA’s Maryland affiliate and several residents. Per Reuters (via Yahoo, read) and CBS News (read), the 2023 law bars guns at places including college campuses, hospitals, power plants, amusement parks, and venues serving alcohol or cannabis on site, on top of older limits at schools, transit, casinos and demonstrations. The Fourth Circuit had upheld every challenged location. Neither article read reports a dissent; the order list itself was not read and the Court’s docket page had not yet updated. Bloomberg and NBC News carried it too (headlines). A denial is not a ruling on the merits.

Also new since Thursday. Florida’s Attorney General issued an opinion (Friday 11/13) calling the state’s 2018 bump-stock ban unconstitutional and said his office will no longer defend convictions under it; the statute stays on the books (Section 02). DOJ’s Second Amendment Section sued Glendale, California over carry-permit costs it puts near $2,000 (Section 03). Texas prefiling for the 2027 session opened 11/9 with about 35 firearm bills on day one, per NRA-ILA (Section 02). In the AR-15 cases, mootness briefing is now complete and the Court has not acted; argument is 2 December (T−16).

Not executed today: the six Google News feeds (robots rule, same as Thursday); Federal Register; Congress.gov; Ballotpedia; the order-list PDF. Also not reachable this run: the News folder on the computer (state file, rolling dashboard and local archive; see the footer).

Why it matters for RTB. Where you can carry is the practical story again. Maryland’s location list stands, Montgomery County’s 100-yard rule stands, and Portland’s building rule is coming. A calm “know before you go” checklist for carrying near parks, hospitals, campuses, transit and venues that serve alcohol fits every state and needs no position on the litigation. Not legal advice.

New ▲ Florida · AG opinion · bump stocks Heat76▲ 11/13 · WUSF and News4Jax read · opinion not read

Florida’s Attorney General calls the state’s 2018 bump-stock ban unconstitutional and says his office will no longer defend convictions under it

On Friday 13 November the Attorney General issued a formal opinion that Fla. Stat. §790.222, the bump-stock ban passed in the 2018 post-Parkland public-safety law, violates the Second Amendment, and said his office “will not defend a conviction under the statute going forward” (News4Jax, WUSF, both read). Both outlets say the statute has not been repealed and remains on the books; no court has ruled it unconstitutional. WUSF notes the 2024 Supreme Court ruling that struck down the federal bump-stock rule did not affect Florida’s state ban. The signer of the 2018 law has defended it, per WUSF. This is the second such Florida AG action in a week, after the 11/6 memo on short-barreled rifles and shotguns.

RTB angleAn AG’s opinion is not a repeal. Florida members should hear it plainly: the statute still exists, local prosecutors and police are not bound by the AG’s appellate position at trial, and possession can still lead to an arrest. A short “what an attorney general opinion does and doesn’t change” explainer is the empowering move. Not legal advice.

New ▲ Supreme Court · cert denied · Maryland Heat86▲ 11/16 order list · Reuters and CBS read · order list not read

Supreme Court declines Novotny v. Moore; Maryland’s sensitive-places carry restrictions stay in force

The petition (No. 25-1324) challenged ten categories of places in Maryland’s carry restrictions, including the 2023 additions: daycare and medical facilities, government buildings, college campuses, power plants, amusement parks, and places “where the public gathers” for entertainment, education or social activities, plus venues serving alcohol or cannabis on site (CBS; Reuters). A district court had blocked a few provisions (public demonstrations; alcohol venues); the Fourth Circuit upheld all of them. SCOTUSblog had reported the case relisted after the 6 November conference, and the petitioners had alternatively asked the Court to send it back in light of Wolford v. Lopez. Neither article read reports a dissent from denial; the order list itself was not read, so any written dissent is unconfirmed. AmmoLand’s headline describes a larger batch of gun-case denials (“22 gun rights cases”); the page refused automated fetches and the count and date are unverified.

RTB anglePractical, not political. For Maryland members, today’s order means the location list is the law. For everyone else, it is a reminder that “sensitive places” vary widely by state and city. Our strongest content is a clear checklist and a reminder that coverage helps when an honest mistake happens. Not legal advice.

AR-15 cases: mootness briefing is complete and the Court has not ruled; argument stays 2 December

The Supreme Court docket in Viramontes v. Cook County (25-238, read) shows the petitioners’ response to the county’s suggestion of mootness (11/11) and the county’s reply (11/13), with no order on the 29 October motion to dismiss Mr. Viramontes or on mootness. The Reload ran a member analysis, podcast and newsletter on the case 11/15 (headlines).

RTB angleSet expectations. Argument is not a decision; a ruling is expected by about June, and the Court could still dispose of Viramontes on procedural grounds. Members’ current state law applies in the meantime. Not legal advice.

New ▲ DOJ · Second Amendment Section · carry permits Heat70▲ Reported 11/12 evening · The Reload read · complaint not read

DOJ sues Glendale, California over carry-permit costs it says can reach about $2,000

The Reload (11/12, read) reports that DOJ’s Civil Rights Division Second Amendment Section filed United States v. Glendale in federal court in the Central District of California. DOJ says the initial application fee is $1,078 and that required fingerprinting, training and psychological-exam fees bring the total to about $2,000. The Reload also reports that the Assistant Attorney General who created the section has resigned, and that the section is pressing ahead regardless. This follows Glendale’s 4 November response deadline to DOJ, which Thursday’s edition flagged. A separate Reload analysis (11/13, headline) says the Montgomery County dismissal “strikes at the heart” of DOJ’s legal strategy; DOJ has not announced an appeal there. Single read source for the Glendale filing.
