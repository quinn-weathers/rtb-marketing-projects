Moved ▲ Courts · Under-21 · Circuit split Heat75▲#6 · citation map complete

Seven courts, four incompatible rationales: the under-21 split now has a citation map, and Succow is the most comprehensive ruling in it

US District Judge Sarala Nagala's 76-page merits opinion in Succow v. Blanche (D. Conn.) upheld the federal statutes barring licensed dealers from selling handguns to adults under 21, Connecticut's broader ban on all pistol sales to that age group including private transfers, and Connecticut's bans on 18-to-20-year-olds possessing or carrying a lawfully acquired handgun. Nagala acknowledged she was the first to reach all of it at once, and rested the federal holding on the founding-era “infancy doctrine” imported from the Fourth Circuit's McCoy v. ATF, plus Reconstruction-era analogues (Wisconsin and Kansas 1883, Nevada 1885) for the state provisions. The opposing authorities are now equally citable: the Fifth Circuit struck the federal purchase ban down in Reese v. ATF (Judge Edith Jones; DOJ declined to appeal), the en banc Eleventh Circuit upheld Florida's total sales ban, a Tenth Circuit panel held in RMGO v. Polis that commercial purchase is not covered by the Second Amendment's plain text at all, and the Eighth and Third Circuits struck down carry bans in Worth v. Jacobson and the Pennsylvania case respectively, both rejecting the infancy doctrine. Succow is not precedential but is appealable to the Second Circuit.

RTB angle: The asset is a clean one-page table: court, case, what it decided, on what reasoning. That is genuinely useful, entirely factual, and nobody else in the coverage space has published it in plain language. Keep the tone level — this is a live constitutional question on which serious judges disagree, and saying so is more credible than pretending it is settled. Handle the subject matter with care: these are restrictions on 18-to-20-year-old adults, and the discussion should not frame young adults adversarially in either direction. The practical member takeaway is simple and worth stating: for now, what an 18-to-20-year-old may lawfully buy, own, or carry depends heavily on which circuit they live in.

USCCA published a defensive-gun-use statistics explainer — sober, hedged, and aimed at the same ground RTB works

Moved → Legal theory · Podcast Public release today (member early access 08/02)

A pro-gun critique of Bruen from the right: Ilya Somin argues the test is “too flabby” to protect the thing it was built to protect

Cato Institute scholar and George Mason law professor Ilya Somin makes an argument on The Reload's podcast that is worth understanding precisely because it cuts against the reflexive read. Somin's position is that Bruen's history-and-tradition framework creates too many grey areas and leaves judges too much room to import their own preferences. He credits liberal critics with a valid structural point while inverting their worry: where they fear the test will be used to strike down government action, he fears it will be used to narrow Second Amendment protection — particularly if the Court's balance shifts. He favors a test grounded in the Second Amendment's original meaning, in which history informs meaning but historical laws that contradict that meaning do not override it. Somin says he has largely agreed with the outcomes of recent Second Amendment cases; his concern is durability, not results so far. Full episode audio, video, and an auto-generated transcript are all posted.

RTB angle: This is a credibility asset. A short piece that fairly presents an internal disagreement among people who all support gun rights signals that RTB is confident enough to discuss method rather than only outcomes, and it is genuinely interesting to an engaged member. Present it as an argument that serious people on the same side make, note the counterargument that Bruen's historical grounding is precisely what makes it hard for a future court to water down, and let readers weigh it. Do not editorialize toward a verdict — the value here is showing the reader the debate exists.
