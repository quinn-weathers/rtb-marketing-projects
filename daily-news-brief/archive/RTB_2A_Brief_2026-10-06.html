Top of mind10 · 06

A day after the Supreme Court passed on reciprocity, an Oklahoma Guard member deployed to Washington faces a D.C. felony for carrying a pistol that is legal at home. Know the law where you are going.

What happened. A 22-year-old Oklahoma National Guard specialist on the federal deployment in Washington was stopped by U.S. Park Police in late September over an expired registration. He was off duty, out of uniform and in his own vehicle. According to the federal affidavit quoted by AmmoLand (read), he told officers he had a pistol, that Oklahoma allows carry without a permit, and that he had no District licence. He was charged with carrying a pistol without a licence, a D.C. felony punishable by up to five years, appeared in D.C. Superior Court on 29 September and was released. AmmoLand reports he has no criminal record. Military.com (10/3), The Truth About Guns (10/5) and Ponca City Now carried the story: four outlets. The case is pending, and the charge is an allegation.

Why today. Yesterday’s denial of Johnson v. Jacobson is now confirmed on the docket itself (“Oct 05 2026 DENIED”). The national reciprocity bill (H.R. 38) was reported by House Judiciary in October 2025 and still awaits a floor vote, per AmmoLand. Congress is out until after the election. Nothing about carrying across state lines has changed, and this case shows what that means in practice: a home-state right does not travel, and the District does not recognise out-of-state permits or permitless carry.

Why it matters for RTB. This is the most concrete illustration of the reciprocity patchwork the category has seen this year, and it involves no use of force at all. The empowering message is preparation, not fear: check the law at your destination before you travel, and know what to say, and not say, at a traffic stop. Do not comment on this individual’s guilt, and do not give legal advice in copy.

Moved ▶ California · SB 948 · safety certificates Heat64▲▲ chaptered text read · signed 9/26 · operative 2028–2029

California’s training change, read in the law: a 4-hour course with live fire for a safety certificate, starting 1 January 2029

The chaptered text of SB 948 (approved and filed 26 September, read on leginfo) does two things. First, training: an applicant for a firearm safety certificate on or after 1 January 2029 must have completed, within the prior year, a course of at least four hours that includes safety and handling instruction and live-fire exercises on a range. Today’s test-only certificate is unchanged until then; the Department of Justice may write implementing regulations. Second, people moving to California with firearms: the reporting window for a “personal firearm importer” grows from 60 to 180 days; from 1 January 2028 the importer must certify, under penalty of perjury, that they hold a safety certificate or an exemption; and bringing a firearm in without obtaining one within 180 days becomes an infraction. The other seven identified bills (AB 2047 3D printers, SB 1220 10-year prohibition, AB 1743, AB 1943, AB 1974, AB 2378) are still summarised from NSSF, not read at source.

RTB angleThe “what changes for California gun owners, and when” explainer can now be written for SB 948. Lead with the dates: nothing changes for buyers until 2029, and new residents have a 2028 step. State what the law requires; don’t characterise the package.
