Top of mind09 · 21

The Justice Department has told Minnesota it will sue over the state’s permit-to-purchase law unless the state stops enforcing it — and it wants an answer by Wednesday. This is the first time the Civil Rights Division’s Second Amendment campaign has aimed at a purchase-permitting scheme rather than a carry-permitting one, and it is the clearest signal yet of where the next phase of the national gun-law fight is heading.

What happened. On Thursday, September 17, the Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights sent a pre-suit letter to Minnesota’s governor and to the mayors of Minneapolis and St. Paul, stating that “Minnesota’s licensing scheme imposes unreasonable barriers to residents’ lawful exercise of their right to acquire and bear arms.” The letter, reported Sunday, does not attack the requirement to hold a purchase permit at all — it attacks how long the permit takes. DOJ says approval waits from the police chiefs in the two largest cities “commonly exceed the statutory limit of 30 days,” with some applicants waiting “nearly 60,” and that the wait applies even after an applicant has already cleared a NICS background check. The legal hook is Bruen’s own caution against regimes where “lengthy wait times in processing license applications… deny ordinary citizens their right to public carry.” What it is not. No complaint has been filed. Nothing is enjoined. Minnesota’s Report of Transfer and Transferee Permit requirements are in force today and every applicant must still comply with them in full. DOJ offered to defer filing if the state and the two cities immediately cease enforcement, concede unconstitutionality and accept a court-enforceable consent decree — terms the reporting outlet reads as unlikely to be accepted — and asked for an answer by Wednesday, September 23. And the threatened suit would rest on the same § 12601 pattern-or-practice theory a California federal judge already called “not obviously sound” in July. Why it matters beyond Minnesota. Permit-to-purchase is the fallback policy most likely to spread if the Supreme Court strikes down “assault weapon” bans in the December case — the same substitution dynamic that produced sensitive-places laws after Bruen. Washington’s own permit-to-purchase mandate takes effect 1 May 2027, which makes this board’s longest-standing unclaimed content slot suddenly current. Also this run: the gun reviewer subpoenaed in the Highland Park product-liability case gave his own account of the subpoena on the record, and the USCCA blog became readable again after a run of provenance blocking — showing ordinary cadence and no peer product, pricing or coverage move on day twenty-one.

    SSJC v. Montgomery County — the injunction still covers one synagogue and one rabbi

    Down three on decay. The reporting outlet returned to the ruling in its own weekend podcast and newsletter, but that is the same outlet revisiting its own story, not a second-outlet pickup, and it is not credited as one. The posture is unchanged: a federal judge in the District of Maryland granted a preliminary injunction barring Montgomery County from enforcing its firearm restrictions against the Silver Spring Jewish Center and its rabbi, and expressly declined to block enforcement against anyone else. The two challenged provisions bar licensed owners from possessing a firearm within 100 yards of a place of worship, and within 100 yards of places of public assembly including schools, parks and libraries. The court found a likelihood of success on the as-applied Second Amendment claim and on the free-exercise claim, citing a Ninth Circuit observation that no colony, state or territory banned firearms at places of worship until after the Fourteenth Amendment, and finding the County’s historical analogues neither close, nor early, nor well-accepted. Two constitutional claims are unresolved, nothing was held unconstitutional, and the judge flagged that further Supreme Court and circuit guidance may change the result. A separate DOJ suit against the same county’s wider gun-free-zone mandates is still pending.
