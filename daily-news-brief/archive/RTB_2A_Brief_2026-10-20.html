Day five in Bend: a second, civil track has opened. The family of the youngest victim has hired three law firms to examine the school’s security, the gun’s purchase and how the attacker got it. Students return Wednesday.

What is new since Monday. The Source (Bend, 10/19, read) reports that the parents of the kindergartner killed at Silver Rail have retained three firms to run a civil investigation, “separate from the criminal one.” Per the firms’ statement, it will look at whether security and active-shooter protocols were in place and followed, whether there were prior warnings, where the weapon was purchased and how it was marketed, and how the 16-year-old got the gun and into the school. The Associated Press version ran in more than 20 outlets overnight (headlines). Separately, Bend police said nine people (four staff, five children) were in the targeted classroom, that another student and adult escaped unharmed, and that evidence collection is finished and the school is back with the district (OPB, KTVZ, KGW, KPTV, Central Oregon Daily; headlines). Investigators also warned against misinformation (KEX, headline).

What is still not confirmed. No charging decision has been reported about anyone. National outlets citing unnamed sources (CBS, AOL; headlines) report new details about how the shotgun came to the attacker; we leave them out because no one has been charged. CNN (headline) asks whether the attacker’s parents could face charges, and Central Oregon Daily (headline) explains what Oregon law says about a 16-year-old having a shotgun. Police have still not confirmed, in anything we read, the intervener’s license status.

The school-safety debate has its own momentum now. The district told The Source (read) that no school resource officer was at Silver Rail (it has eight for 33 schools), that police will step up patrols near schools, and that it will run a “top to bottom” review. A KATU report on Oregon’s school security requirements was syndicated to at least eight stations. On the other side, a Crime Prevention Research Center poll on armed teachers (via AmmoLand, read), a Hawaii editor and a Washington Times op-ed question no-carry school rules (Section 02).

Why it matters for RTB. Recommendation, unchanged in substance: keep Bend and everyone involved out of all marketing, keep evergreen content running, and do not publish a “what happens after” explainer before Thursday, the day after students return. When it runs, keep it general. Today’s development gives it a clearer core: criminal and civil cases are separate tracks, with different questions, different standards and different timelines, and either can follow any use of force. That is an empowerment message, not a fear message: knowing the two tracks exist is how people prepare for them. Not legal advice; no one named.

New ▲ Waiting periods · Maine · New Mexico Heat76▲▲ NSSF release read 10/20 · AmmoLand read · 9+ outlets

A day after the Supreme Court left Maine’s 72-hour wait in place, NSSF filed a new suit over New Mexico’s seven-day wait

New Mexico (new, 10/20). Per NSSF’s release (read), the trade association filed a lawsuit seeking declaratory and injunctive relief against New Mexico’s attorney general over continued enforcement of the state’s seven-day “cooling off” period. The background: in Ortega v. Grisham the Tenth Circuit held the law infringes the Second Amendment for buyers who have passed a NICS check, and on remand the district court entered a permanent injunction covering the named plaintiffs. The state is appealing again and, per NSSF, has said it will enforce the law, including against licensed retailers not party to that case. The complaint itself, the court and the case number were not in what we read.

Maine (follow-up). Monday’s denial in Beckwith v. Frey (No. 26-258) drew at least nine outlets. AmmoLand (read) notes the case reached the Court at the preliminary-injunction stage: the First Circuit reversed an injunction in April, holding that buying a gun is not covered by the Amendment’s plain text, and the litigation continues in the lower courts. Its account of a split with the Tenth Circuit is the challengers’ argument as well as AmmoLand’s.

RTB angleTwo states, two answers, one practical message. Buyers in Maine still wait 72 hours after a background check; in New Mexico the law’s reach is being fought over case by case. Members who buy for urgent self-protection should know their state’s rule before they need it, and plan around it. A denial is not a ruling on the merits. Not legal advice.

Moved ▶ ATF · NFA · day eleven Heat78▼ FR API checked 10/20 · Duke analysis read

Day eleven of NFA non-enforcement, still with no rule; a September Third Circuit ruling that upheld silencer registration is a reminder of the legal ground underneath

The Federal Register API (read 10/20) still shows zero ATF documents published since 1 October, so the change remains guidance plus an injunction. Late find, not a new event: in United States v. Berger, decided 1 September, a Third Circuit panel upheld a man’s convictions for possessing machine guns and unregistered silencers. Per the Duke Center’s analysis (10/14, read), the majority held that the NFA’s registration requirements do not “infringe” the Second Amendment right even if silencers are “arms.” The case concerned the law as it then applied, before the current non-enforcement, but it shows that at least one appeals court sees registration itself as constitutional. Industry: GrabAGun issued a statement on the ATF guidance (Business Wire, 10/20) and NSSF scheduled a webinar on the new NFA landscape (headlines; Section 04). Not executed this run: the N.D. Tex. and E.D. Mo. dockets.

RTB angleDurability is still the message. Guidance can be withdrawn, injunctions can be narrowed, and courts have upheld registration. Buyers should keep records of how and when they acquired NFA items, and check state law first. Not legal advice.

Women made up nearly half of recent new gun owners, and their share of all owners rose from 22% to 32%, per a RAND analysis summarized by NSSF

RAND, as quoted, says women in nearly every demographic group increased ownership. NSSF ties the trend to personal protection and cites growing Atlanta-area clubs for Black women. Caveats: we read NSSF’s summary, not RAND’s report; the “self-defense” explanation is NSSF’s framing.

RTB angleUsable and on-brand, after this week. New owners who chose a gun for protection are exactly the people who benefit from learning what happens after a defensive incident.
