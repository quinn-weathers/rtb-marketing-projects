Top of mind10 · 27

Starting 27 November, licensed gun dealers can run their own employees and job applicants through the FBI’s background-check system. The Justice Department published the final “firearm handler check” rule in today’s Federal Register, four years after Congress authorized it.

What happened. Per the Federal Register entry (read through the FR API), DOJ is amending its NICS regulations (28 CFR part 25; Docket FBI-159) so federal firearms licensees may voluntarily run “firearm handler background checks” on certain current or prospective employees. The rule is effective 27 November 2026. Until now, NICS could be used only for prospective buyers. The NSSF release (read) says the provision traces to the Bipartisan Safer Communities Act of 2022, that the industry pressed for it since, and that it is meant to keep prohibited persons away from dealer inventory. ATF itself has published nothing in the Federal Register since 1 October.

What is not confirmed. We read the rule’s summary, not its full text. Which employees count as “eligible,” what consent the employee must give, and how results are handled are in the rule body we have not yet read. Only NSSF has commented so far.

Also this morning. Massachusetts mail-ballot requests are due today; a WWLP report on gun-registration deadlines has spread to three other outlets, but we could not open it (Section 02). The Supreme Court’s 6 November conference now lists 24 firearms cases, and the first amicus brief in the AR-15 cases was filed yesterday (Section 03). Ohio and Alaska lawmakers are moving to align state suppressor law with the federal change (Section 02).

Why it matters for RTB. This is a quiet, practical safety measure the industry asked for. It does not change anything for buyers or carriers. For members who work at or own a shop, a short “what the new employee-check rule means for your store” note (voluntary, effective 27 November, read the rule before relying on it) is useful and uncontroversial. Not legal advice.

New ▲ DOJ / FBI · NICS · final rule Heat71▲▲ FR 2026-21920 · effective 11/27 · NSSF read

Final rule lets licensed dealers voluntarily run “firearm handler” background checks on employees through NICS, starting 27 November

The rule’s Federal Register summary (read) says DOJ is amending its NICS regulations (28 CFR part 25; AG Order 7257-2026) so FFLs may use NICS “for purposes of voluntarily conducting employment background checks” on certain current or prospective employees. Effective 27 November 2026 (T−31). NSSF (read) calls it a long-sought industry priority under the 2022 Bipartisan Safer Communities Act, aimed at keeping prohibited persons from working around dealer inventory, and notes the same law lets dealers check serial numbers against stolen-gun records. The full rule text (eligibility, consent, record handling) was not read.

RTB angleNothing changes for buyers or carriers. It is a voluntary tool for shops. Useful for members who own or work at a dealership; otherwise, low-priority content. Describe it as written; no characterization of who supported or opposed it. Not legal advice.
