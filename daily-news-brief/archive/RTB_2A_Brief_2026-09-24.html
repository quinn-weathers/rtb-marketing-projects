Top of mind09 · 24

The under-21 handgun opinion finally has its second outlet, its primary document and a named official on the record — and the first sign that it may change how the Justice Department argues in court. The check this board could not run for three straight days has now been run, and it came back confirmed.

What happened. On Wednesday evening The Reload reported the Office of Legal Counsel opinion that this board has carried since 09/17 on a single outlet. It independently confirms the core: OLC concluded that applying 18 U.S.C. §§ 922(b)(1) and (c)(1) — the Gun Control Act provisions that bar licensed dealers from selling handguns to 18-to-20-year-olds — to otherwise law-abiding young adults violates the Second Amendment. It links the memorandum opinion on justice.gov (located, not fetched — see the footer) and quotes Assistant Attorney General T. Elliot Gaiser on the record that the Department “may not constitutionally seek to impose criminal penalties on dealers” in those sales. The genuinely new element: the Firearms Policy Coalition, which is suing over the same restriction in Reese v. ATF, said the Department asked for an extension in that case immediately after the opinion was published and that the judge granted it. That is reported second-hand from the plaintiff’s own post and the docket was not reached, but if accurate it is the first observable sign that the opinion may shift the government’s litigating position.

What it does not do. The same report is explicit that an OLC opinion is non-binding executive-branch advice: it does not invalidate the statute, does not bind courts, Congress or the states, and does not by itself change what a dealer may lawfully do. The provisions remain in the United States Code, and no ATF guidance to dealers has reached this board — that half of the standing assignment is still unexecuted, because atf.gov remains unreachable. Reaction split along familiar lines: Everytown Law called the move a dereliction of duty; the National Association for Gun Rights called it the right direction while saying it does not yet know what the Department will do with it.

Why it matters for RTB. This is the week’s cleanest example of the gap between a headline (“DOJ says under-21 ban is unconstitutional”) and a member’s actual legal position (nothing has changed at the counter yet). That gap is RTB’s lane. A short process explainer — what an OLC opinion is, what it binds, what would have to happen next — is now fully sourceable. Separately, USCCA has started threading its new law-firm structure into its evergreen content library (section 04) — a messaging move, not a product change, and the first time USCCA Law has appeared on the blog index this board reads.
