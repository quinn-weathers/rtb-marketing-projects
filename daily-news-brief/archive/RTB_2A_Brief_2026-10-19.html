Top of mind10 · 19

Four days after the Bend school attack, the legal questions are turning to how the shotgun was obtained, not to the parent who stopped the attacker. Bend police said they would release more details today.

What is new since Friday. The man who followed the 16-year-old into Silver Rail Elementary and shot him has been publicly identified by his family as the father of two students (ABC News, NBC News, The Source; one read). Police, as quoted by CBS and Central Oregon Daily (headlines), also credit a teacher who threw a phone at the attacker and fought him. PBS and The New York Times (headlines) report the attacker was a former student who targeted a special-education classroom. The Source (Bend, 10/19, read) reports that the Deschutes County District Attorney’s office would not confirm details, that police promised more information today, and that whether anyone else faces charges, for example for criminal negligence, will depend on what prosecutors find. Its reporting focuses on the purchase of the shotgun by a family member; national outlets citing unnamed officials say the gun was registered to her. Bend-La Pine schools reopen Wednesday; Silver Rail stays closed indefinitely.

What is still not confirmed. Police have not confirmed, in anything we read, that the parent held a concealed handgun license (USA Carry and Fox News report it, citing federal sources), and officials have still not said how the district’s no-carry policy applies. No charging decision has been reported about anyone.

The debate has widened. Oregon outlets (OregonLive, KTVZ, The Source) now frame the attack against Oregon’s gun laws and the voter-approved Measure 114, which is still awaiting an Oregon Supreme Court ruling. The New York Times (headline) expects it to sharpen the governor’s race. On the other side, the Second Amendment Foundation’s counsel proposed school-security changes including voluntary armed staff and hardened doors (TTAG, 10/18), and the NRA issued a statement on the armed citizen (Outdoor Wire, 10/19).

Why it matters for RTB. The weekend pause did its job. Recommendation for this week: resume scheduled evergreen content, keep Bend and everyone involved out of all marketing, and hold any “what happens after a defensive shooting” explainer until at least Wednesday. When it runs, keep it general, without reference to this case. The empowerment-led point holds: even a widely praised defensive act leads to an investigation, questions about where a gun was carried, and a personal cost. Being prepared means knowing the rules where you carry and having support lined up beforehand. Not legal advice; no one named.

New ▲ DOJ · ATF · private sales notice filed 10/9 · reported 10/16 · TTAG read

DOJ appeals the ruling that struck down the “engaged in the business” rule; ATF says it wants a narrower version, not the full rule

Per TTAG (10/16, read), DOJ filed a notice of appeal to the Fifth Circuit on 9 October in Texas v. ATF, challenging the district court’s June final judgment and August order. It raises three questions: the plaintiffs’ standing; whether only businesses that make a profit, or that have completed sales, can be “engaged in the business”; and whether vacating the whole rule was wrong. GOA criticised the appeal. ATF’s director told News2A.com that the agency’s proposal removes the 2024 rule’s added presumptions and keeps narrower provisions, such as definitions of “purchase” and “sale” and guidance for estate-sale auctioneers. He also said it would drop limits on former licensees’ occasional private sales. Late find, not a new event: the notice is ten days old. The notice itself was not read, and no other outlet surfaced on our query.

RTB anglePrivate sellers: the rules are still moving. The line between an occasional private sale and dealing without a licence is being re-argued in court and in rulemaking at the same time. Keep records of private sales. Not legal advice.

New ▲ Data · Gun Violence Archive 10/19 · TTAG read · GVA snapshot 10/13

Third-quarter shooting deaths are the lowest in over a decade of GVA tracking, while estimated gun sales are up about 2% this year

Per TTAG (10/19, read), citing the Gun Violence Archive’s 13 October snapshot: 9,790 shooting deaths and 18,199 injuries through three quarters, with Q3’s 3,301 deaths the lowest third quarter in GVA’s records. Mass shootings rose, to 354 from 327 at the same point last year (GVA’s four-or-more definition), though they account for about 3.6% of shooting deaths. Estimated sales, based on adjusted NICS data, are about 10.6 million through Q3, up roughly 2%. Caveats: GVA’s running count currently excludes suicides, about two-thirds of gun deaths in CDC data. The sales estimates are adjusted NICS figures, not actual sales. TTAG’s explanation of the trends is its own.

RTB angleUse the numbers carefully, if at all, this week. “Fewer people are being shot” is true and empowering, but it sits awkwardly next to Bend. Quote GVA directly, include the suicide caveat, and hold until next week.
