Top of mind10 · 07

The Justice Department has told a federal court that lawful self-defense carry is allowed in public national-park buildings, but the Park Service’s own guidance still says it is not. Until the two agree, members are getting mixed signals from the federal government.

What happened. In a 2 October motion to dismiss in Zimmerman v. Blanche (N.D. Tex., No. 4:26-cv-00372), DOJ argues the plaintiffs lack standing because they face no credible threat of prosecution under 18 U.S.C. § 930(a) for the carry they describe: a lawfully possessed handgun, carried for self-defense, inside park facilities that are generally open to the public, such as visitor centers and shops. According to AmmoLand (10/6, read), DOJ relies on the § 930(d)(3) “other lawful purposes” exception and on the Office of Legal Counsel’s 12 August opinion on post offices, which also criticised making park visitors disarm at a building door. The FPC case page (read) confirms the 10/2 filing. AmmoLand reports that the National Park Service’s firearms page, as of 6 October, still describes a prohibition in visitor centers, ranger stations and other facilities. The plaintiffs’ response is due around 23 October under the court’s ordinary 21-day rule (AmmoLand’s estimate).

What it is not. This is a litigation position, not a ruling, injunction or change to the statute or NPS rules. It does not cover federal courthouses, secured or non-public areas, or anyone who cannot lawfully possess a firearm, and state and local law still apply where park boundaries cross jurisdictions. We read AmmoLand’s account and the case page, not the motion itself.

Why it matters for RTB. It is the third “carry while travelling” story in three days, after the D.C. Guard charge (10/6) and FPC’s new Denver nonresident suit (section 02). The member question is the same each time: whose rules apply where I am standing? The empowering answer is to check before you go, follow posted rules until the agency itself updates them, and know who to call if something goes wrong. Don’t tell members they may now carry in park buildings.

New ▲ Colorado · Denver · nonresident carry Heat62▲▲ filed 9/29 · late find · 2 outlets

FPC sues Denver and Colorado, arguing visitors from 15 states have no lawful way to carry in the city

Hoffman v. City and County of Denver was filed in the U.S. District Court for Colorado on 29 September by the Firearms Policy Coalition and a member, per ConcealedCarry.com (10/6, read); TTAG also reported it. The complaint says three rules stack up: Colorado issues carry permits only to residents; it honours out-of-state permits only from states with reciprocity, which excludes 15 states and D.C. (including California, New York, Illinois, Washington and South Carolina); and Denver bans open carry. It raises Second Amendment and Article IV Privileges and Immunities claims. It is the second challenge to the same gap, after the NRA and the Colorado State Shooting Association’s Howeth suit (31 August). ConcealedCarry.com’s president, who wrote the piece, notes that similar wins in California, New York and Hawaii produced nonresident permit processes, not reciprocity. That is the author’s analysis; the complaint itself was not read here.

RTB angleA clean “planning a trip to Denver?” explainer. Today, a visitor from a non-reciprocal state has no lawful carry option in Denver; the lawsuit could change that, but hasn’t yet. Use official state sources for the reciprocity list. No prediction about the outcome.

New ▲ N.D. Tex. · Zimmerman v. Blanche · national parks Heat66▲▲ motion to dismiss 10/2 · response ~10/23

DOJ says lawful self-defense carry is permitted in public national-park buildings; the Park Service page has not caught up

See Top of mind. In brief: DOJ’s 2 October motion to dismiss argues that, under the August OLC opinion, carrying a lawfully possessed handgun for self-defense into park facilities generally open to the public falls within § 930(d)(3)’s “lawful purposes” exception, so the plaintiffs face no credible prosecution threat. DOJ also says the compendia for the 15 parks the lead plaintiff plans to visit contain no § 1.5 firearm restrictions; that claim covers those parks only. The plaintiffs are Gary Zimmerman, Lisa Ventura, FPC and the Second Amendment Foundation, represented by Cooper & Kirk (FPC case page, read). AmmoLand reports the NPS firearms page still lists visitor centers among prohibited facilities.

RTB angleExplain the mixed signal; don’t resolve it for members. Follow posted rules and current NPS guidance until the agency changes them or a court rules. A natural fit for a “travelling with a firearm: federal land” explainer.

New ▲ ATF · April deregulation package · comments Heat63▲▲ analysis read · final rules not yet published

The Trace’s count of ~38,000 comments: travel and brace changes drew near-unanimous support; direct-to-door shipping and records changes drew opposition, including from NSSF

The Trace (10/7, read) used AI models to classify most of the public comments on 28 of the ~34 ATF changes proposed in April. Twenty-one proposals drew at least 90% support, including easing travel across state lines with a firearm (stops at a hotel or gas station; 4,000+ comments, nearly all supportive) and removing pistol-brace restrictions. Three drew mostly negative comments: allowing dealers to ship guns directly to in-state buyers after a remote identity and background check (about three-quarters of 3,500+ comments opposed, including NSSF, which cited a member survey showing 72% opposed and straw-purchase risk); making electronic dealer records the norm (nearly 90% of 580+ opposed, on registry concerns); and letting dealers discard records after 20 or 30 years (nearly 80% of 5,700+ opposed, much of it a GOA form letter arguing for less retention). Most comment periods closed in early August. ATF must review comments before issuing final rules; the Federal Register (checked) shows none yet. The Trace discloses grant funding from Everytown’s non-political arm; a companion Trace piece (headline) frames this as pushback “from an unlikely source.”

RTB angleThe travel safe-harbor is the member-facing change to watch. It is proposed, not final; nothing changes for travellers until ATF publishes a final rule. Note the industry split on direct shipping as market intelligence only; take no position.
