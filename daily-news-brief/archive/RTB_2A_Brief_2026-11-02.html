Voters decide five firearms-related ballot questions tomorrow, and ATF’s chief counsel, the architect of its 34-rule rollback package, has resigned. Nothing changes for gun owners today; both stories set up Wednesday’s questions.

What happened. ATF: the agency announced on Friday, 30 October, that Chief Counsel Robert Leider has resigned and is expected to return to teaching law at George Mason University; its release credits him with supporting “more than 40 proposed and final rules” (ATF, read). The Reload (read) calls him the architect of the 34-rule package announced in May, which is still in rulemaking, and expects most of it to be finalized but says the most contested proposal (letting dealers complete some sales without an in-person visit, opposed by NSSF) is the likeliest to be withdrawn. Ballots: Massachusetts Question 9 (a “yes” keeps the 2024 law), Arkansas Issue 2 (right-to-bear-arms amendment), Missouri Amendment 8 (elected sheriffs), Idaho’s advisory state-gun question, and a Lea County, New Mexico, tax for a legal-defense fund.

What is not confirmed. Why Leider left: The Reload says the split looks amicable but could not confirm the full story. Who replaces him has not been announced. Which rules, if any, will be dropped is analysis, not an agency statement. The Massachusetts “register today” claim remains unverified.

Also this morning. Portland’s own release confirms the ban covers City Hall, the Portland Building and the 1900 Building, and takes effect only once signs and a web list are posted (Section 02; corrects Friday’s statute reference). FPC and NRA sued Pennsylvania over its age-21 carry-license rule, and a Virginia court hears the under-21 handgun challenge on 18 November (Section 03). Florida’s waiting-period ruling came from a federal judge, not a state judge as we reported Friday. Glendale’s DOJ deadline is Wednesday.

Why it matters for RTB. Members will ask “did the law change?” on Wednesday. Have the two-branch explainers ready (Massachusetts: law stays / law repealed; Arkansas: amendment passes / fails), each with a “confirm with the state” line. On ATF, the empowering message is simple: proposed rules are still proposals, nothing changed Friday, and we will tell members when anything final publishes. No forecasts. Not legal advice.

Moved ▶ Oregon · Portland · city buildings Heat80▲ City release read 11/2 · 11 outlets

Portland’s own release: licensed carry ends in City Hall, the Portland Building and the 1900 Building once signs are posted

The City’s 29 October release (read) says the Council voted 12–0 on 28 October to end the exception that let concealed-handgun license holders carry into buildings the City uses for official meetings, naming City Hall, the Portland Building and the 1900 Building. It cites Senate Bill 243, effective 26 September 2025, which lets local governments end the license-holder exception in their meeting buildings (Multnomah County did so in September 2025). Not yet in force: as SB 243 requires, no designation takes effect until signs are posted at every normal entrance and the City publishes a list of covered buildings. The City Administrator will set a “progressive enforcement” procedure (notice, a chance to cure, exclusion from the building) before any police referral; the release says the ordinance creates no new offenses or penalties. Weekend commentary: Seattle Red (11/2), PJ Media (10/30), the Oregon Firearms Federation.

RTB angleThe “know before you go” piece now has its facts. Look for posted signs at government buildings; a license is not a pass into every building; Oregon law now lets more local bodies opt in. The “notice and a chance to cure” detail is useful and calming. No position on the ordinance. Not legal advice.

03ATF, courts & regulatory

Rules, rulings, enforcement
New ▲ ATF · leadership · rule package Heat84▲▲ 10/30 release · The Reload read · 4 outlets

ATF’s chief counsel resigns; The Reload expects most of the 34-rule package to survive, but not the contested no-visit sales rule

ATF announced on 30 October that Chief Counsel Robert Leider has resigned and is expected to return to the Antonin Scalia Law School at George Mason University. The release says he joined in early 2025 and supported “more than 40 proposed and final rules” (read). The Reload’s analysis (11/1, read) describes him as the architect of the 34-rule package announced in May, still moving through rulemaking. It reports public statements from both sides describing an amicable split, but says it could not confirm the full story. Its assessment: most rules will likely be finalized because they are modest and durable; the proposal most at risk is “Revising Non-Over-the-Counter Firearms Transaction Requirements,” which would let dealers complete some sales without an in-store visit using approved online identity checks, and which drew heavy opposition from small dealers and NSSF. A rumored broader “sporting purposes” import rule, it says, probably will not come. The Truth About Guns, AmmoLand and Guns.com also reported the departure. No successor named.

RTB angleA staffing change, not a law change. Proposed rules remain proposals; nothing changed for members on Friday. If members ask: final rules publish in the Federal Register with effective dates, and we will flag them. Do not forecast which rules survive. Not legal advice.

Moved ▶ Waiting periods · Florida · New Mexico Heat75▲ 10/29–31 · USA Carry read · 3 outlets

Florida’s waiting-period ruling was federal: the judge refused a joint judgment and invited state officials, Brady and Giffords to weigh in

Florida (Dunn v. Glass, U.S. District Court, Middle District of Florida): per USA Carry (read), the court denied the parties’ joint motion to enter a judgment declaring the state’s three-day waiting period unconstitutional beyond background-check time, citing the lack of a real adversarial contest and a remedy beyond the parties’ power. It invited the Governor, both legislative chambers, Brady and Giffords to participate as amici: notices due 30 November (T−28), amicus briefs 1 February 2027, party briefs 19 February 2027. The waiting period stays in effect. State Affairs Pro and AmmoLand also reported it. New Mexico (NSSF v. Torrez, D.N.M.): SAF, NSSF, NRA and co-plaintiffs moved on 22 October for a preliminary injunction against the seven-day wait for their members and customers; the state says it enforces the law against everyone except the two Ortega plaintiffs who won in August. No ruling; the state has not responded (USA Carry, 10/31, read).

New ▲ Under-21 · Pennsylvania · Virginia Heat73▲▲ 10/29–11/2 · USA Carry read · 3 outlets

FPC and NRA sue Pennsylvania over its age-21 carry-license rule; a Virginia court hears the under-21 handgun and background-check challenge on 18 November

Pennsylvania (Maines v. Bethel, U.S. District Court, Eastern District of Pennsylvania, filed 29 October): FPC, NRA and a Philadelphia resident aged 18 to 20 challenge the age-21 requirement for a license to carry (18 Pa.C.S. §6109(b)), suing the Philadelphia police commissioner and the acting State Police commissioner. They seek a declaration, an injunction, and an order stopping the state background system from blocking licenses for 18-to-20-year-olds; they rely on the Third Circuit’s 2025 Lara decision, which the Supreme Court declined to review in June (USA Carry, read; Hoodline; FPC release via Outdoor Wire). Virginia (Boyer v. Katz, Lynchburg Circuit Court, a state court): GOA, VCDL and two individuals challenge the new under-21 handgun purchase ban and the universal background-check law; hearing 18 November (T−16). Both laws remain in force (USA Carry, 11/1, read). No rulings.
