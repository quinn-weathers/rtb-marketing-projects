Moved ▼ Federal · Both chambers · NFA Heat74▼#5 · 47 signatories · per AmmoLand

The 47-lawmaker letter asked DOJ for something the stay lapse partly delivered — and the harder half of the ask is still outstanding

Forty-seven Republican lawmakers wrote to DOJ before the seven-day stay expired asking the department to stop defending the NFA's zero-tax registration scheme and to direct ATF to apply Judge Hendrix's ruling nationwide. Half of that request effectively resolved itself when DOJ let the stay lapse without appealing: the injunction is now in force as to the people it covers. The other half — nationwide application — did not, and it cannot be delivered by a court. Judge Hendrix was explicit that his order reaches only “the plaintiffs and, where applicable, their agencies, political subdivisions, members, and customers — both current and future.” Everyone outside that circle is relying on nothing at all right now. Only the executive branch can close that gap, by choosing as a matter of enforcement policy not to pursue the enjoined provisions against anyone, and it has not said whether it will. (Signatory count and letter contents per AmmoLand's index dek of its Aug 11 report; the letter itself was not retrieved. Treat the count as reported, not verified.)

RTB angle: This is still the best civics explainer available to RTB, and it has gotten sharper rather than staler. There are now three visibly different things in play and they behave completely differently: a court injunction that binds the government only as to specified people; an enforcement policy that an agency can adopt by memo and a successor can revoke by memo; and a statute, which takes an act of Congress to change and an act of Congress to undo. A member asking “am I covered?” is really asking which of those three they are standing on. Lay the three out plainly, note that RTB is not a law firm and that no membership exempts anyone from federal or state law, and let readers reach their own conclusions about which foundation they would rather build on.

Moved ▲ Federal · Both chambers · S. 1169 / H.R. 2442 verified · Risch, Issa, Hudson · Cornyn cosponsor

Freedom From Unfair Gun Taxes Act verified too — S. 1169 and H.R. 2442, aimed at state excise taxes rather than the federal one

Also carried unverified until today, and also now confirmed from NSSF's own reporting. The Freedom from Unfair Gun Taxes Act is S. 1169 (Sen. Jim Risch, R-Idaho) and H.R. 2442 (Reps. Darrell Issa, R-Calif., and Richard Hudson, R-N.C.), with Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) added as a cosponsor. It would bar states and localities from imposing excise taxes on the sale of firearms, ammunition or components by manufacturers, producers or importers, and it is drafted to leave the federal Pittman-Robertson conservation excise framework untouched. The context: California has an 11 percent state excise tax, Colorado 6.5 percent, and bills have been introduced in Maryland, Massachusetts, New York, Virginia and Washington — Virginia's HB 919 would impose 11 percent on retail gross receipts. NSSF's argument runs through the poll-tax line of cases.

RTB angle: Note the symmetry with the NFA story and use it. The Silencer Shop ruling turned on the proposition that a tax cut to zero dissolved the constitutional footing for a federal registration scheme; this bill argues that states cannot use targeted taxes to burden the same right from the other direction. Same constitutional question, opposite fact pattern, and both live in the same week. That is an unusually clean explainer. Present the state-tax debate as a genuine policy dispute with two sides — supporters of these taxes make a public-cost argument that deserves stating — and keep RTB's frame on what it costs an ordinary person to exercise a right.

Moved ▲▲ Oregon · Measure 114 · Study + status Heat69▲▲#8 · three distinct outlets · operative date moved

The Measure 114 demand study now has named authors, a peer-reviewed journal and a third outlet — and the measure's operative date has quietly slipped to 2028

Yesterday this was a single-outlet statistic folded into the Colorado card. Today it is properly sourced and carries a genuine state-law status update. The research is by Katie Bollman, Benjamin Hansen, Edward Rubin and Garrett Stanford, published in the Journal of Public Economics: firearm-related background checks in Oregon rose 13.9 percent in anticipation of the November 2022 referendum, then 157 percent immediately after voters approved it 50.7 to 49.3, generating roughly 78,000 additional checks between the election and the court order that stopped the law — with about 63,000 still above expected trend eighteen months later. Separately, and materially: Measure 114 still has not taken effect. A Harney County court struck it down under the Oregon Constitution in 2023, the Court of Appeals reversed in March 2025, the Oregon Supreme Court heard argument in November 2025 and has not ruled, and Oregon lawmakers have enacted HB 4145 (Chapter 120) pushing the transfer and magazine provisions to January 1, 2028.

RTB angle: Report the number, resist the temptation to over-read it. A 157 percent surge before a restriction takes effect is a timing effect — people moving purchases forward — and the honest framing is that it tells you about anticipation, not about long-run ownership or about whether the policy works. Say that plainly; it costs nothing and it is the difference between analysis and cheerleading. The three-state pattern is the stronger story anyway: Oregon +157%, Colorado +264% in July ahead of an Aug 1 effective date, Virginia +241% in June ahead of July 1. And the 2028 date is genuinely useful service journalism for Oregon members who may believe the law is already live.

New ▲▲▲ ATF · Guidance vacuum · NFA Heat88▲▲▲#2 · new to board · AmmoLand 08/13

The injunction is live, a licensed dealer is transferring under it, and ATF has told nobody what it thinks the order means
